Reactor Digital Twin · Fault Detection & Diagnosis

Watching a CSTR the way a control room does — then teaching a model to watch it too.

A first-principles simulator of a non-isothermal, jacket-cooled CSTR (the classic Bequette bistable reactor) drives four models that mirror what refining and petrochemical plants actually run in production: a soft sensor for the slow lab assay, a fault classifier, an unsupervised anomaly detector, and a remaining-useful-life estimator for slowly developing faults.

System design

Four models, one plant, one honest evaluation split

Soft sensor

Infer the slow lab assay from fast tags

The concentration analyzer (AT‑104) samples every 15 minutes with a 16‑minute dead time — realistic for a GC or wet-chemistry lab loop. An XGBoost regressor, tuned with Optuna, predicts Ca continuously from temperature, jacket command, flow, and level alone, closing the gap between lab samples.

Fault classifier

Identify which of seven conditions is active

Rolling 20‑minute windows of fast tags are summarized (mean, std, min, max, slope) and classified by a tuned XGBoost model into one of seven fault types or normal operation — the core of an abnormal situation management system.

Anomaly detector

Flag deviations the classifier was never shown

An Isolation Forest trained exclusively on normal‑operation windows scores every window by how unlike normal it looks — a safety net for fault modes outside the labeled catalogue.

RUL predictor

Estimate time-to-saturation for incipient faults

For fouling and catalyst deactivation, remaining useful life is defined as minutes until the PID controller's cooling command saturates and can no longer hold setpoint — a physically grounded failure event, not an arbitrary threshold. Episodes that never saturate are right‑censored and excluded, rather than assigned an invented label.

Notes for reviewers

What this is, and what it isn't

Why simulate instead of using plant data

Real plant historian data is proprietary and almost never comes with clean fault labels or onset times — exactly what's required to evaluate a fault detector rather than just build one. A first‑principles simulator with injected, labeled faults is the same approach used to construct public benchmarks in this space, including the Tennessee Eastman Process dataset.

The reactor is genuinely nonlinear

This CSTR exhibits real open‑loop input multiplicity: a fixed jacket temperature sweep shows a low‑conversion branch and a high‑conversion branch separated by an extinction point near Tc ≈ 305 K. The plant operates on the high‑conversion branch with an engineered safety margin from that cliff — the same kind of constraint a real control system would enforce.

Limitations, stated plainly

  • All models are trained and evaluated on simulator‑generated data. No real plant data has validated any result shown here.
  • Fault severities and onset times are sampled from the same distribution at train and test time — this measures interpolation, not generalization to an unmodeled plant.
  • The RUL predictor's usable accuracy is bounded by how many simulated episodes actually reach saturation within the episode horizon; see the training report for current sample sizes.
